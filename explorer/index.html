How the estimates are made, and how wrong they are

  • Each measured chip and workload becomes one log-efficiency value. The model is log efficiency = workload level + a tier-by-workload shift + a generation effect + a tier effect + a chip-specific term, with ridge priors that shrink the shifts and the chip term toward zero. A chip with no measurements at all, such as the M1, M2 and M5 Ultra, gets only its generation and tier effects.
  • Throughput is fitted the same way. Published scores replace the model where they exist without matching power: the M3 Ultra scores 3,028 to 3,082 in Cinebench 2024 multi-core across reviews, and the M2 Ultra scores 28,550 in Cinebench R23.
  • Leave-one-out validation removes each measured cell in turn, refits, and predicts it. The root-mean-square error of those predictions is the uncertainty shown on every estimate: . Chips with no measurements get 1.5 times that.
  • Apple publishes idle and maximum wall power for each Mac mini and Mac Studio. Maximum power gives a hard floor, work per joule ≥ throughput ÷ maximum power, which clips the lower error bar of some desktop estimates. GPU estimates are not clipped, because Apple's maximum includes CPU and GPU together.
  • Power modes: Digital Trends measured the M4 Pro Mac mini in Low Power, Automatic and High Power mode (Cinebench 2024 multi-core 765, 1,622 and 1,707). Nobody published wall power per mode, so it is estimated from package power: about 46 W in High Power and 40 W in Automatic (Notebookcheck), and a 13.5 W cap in Low Power (Eclectic Light), times 1.15 for conversion losses plus 6 to 10 W for the rest of the machine.

What is missing and what was left out
  • Ultra chips under Cinebench. Reviews in English, German and Japanese give M3 Ultra scores but no wall power for the run. PC Watch reports about 146 W with Cinebench and a game running together. The M3 Ultra is measured only under HPL and LLM generation, where Jeff Geerling metered it at the wall. Its Cinebench points are estimates.
  • Other languages. Notebookcheck's German pages carry the same measurements as the English ones. Geekerwan (Chinese) publishes per-core power curves from package power, not wall power, and in video only. ITmedia and PC Watch (Japanese) give scores without matched wall power. Macwelt (German) gives about 124 W for the M4 Max Mac Studio under combined CPU and GPU load, which is not a single workload.
  • Blender. Render speeds are Blender Open Data medians (Blender 4.5), which pool every machine with a given chip, so a laptop, a Mac mini and a Mac Studio count alike. No source pairs them with metered wall energy, so all Blender points have estimated power: on the CPU, the same machine's Cinebench all-core wall power (0.9 to 1.1 times); on the GPU, 0.45 to 0.95 times its Cyberpunk 2077 wall power, a factor anchored only by one package-power reading. Error bars combine the spread of the median, the power range and the factor in quadrature on a log scale, and are about one standard deviation. The per-scene medians (monster, junkshop, classroom) come from the full Open Data snapshot and reuse the three-scene power. GPU points use each chip's full-core GPU; the cut-down variants in Open Data (for example the 30-core M3 Max or the 16-core M4 Pro) are left out, because no machine with them has a measured or modelled power on this page. Medians over three runs or fewer (M5 Ultra, M6, M5 Pro GPU) are marked as weaker measurements.
  • Other games. Nobody publishes wall power for Baldur's Gate 3 or Assassin's Creed Shadows on these Macs, so those points borrow the Cyberpunk 2077 reading of the same machine. Total War: Pharaoh is left out because it is engine-limited near 50 to 56 fps from the M4 Pro up to the M5 Max, so it says little about the GPU; Shadow of the Tomb Raider because it has no M5, M5 Pro or M5 Max results. Upscaled or frame-generated results (MetalFX) and Geekerwan's figures, which are chip power rather than wall power, are not mixed in.
  • M5 Ultra. The only wall-power figures found came from a blog whose 70B token rates are about 1.5 times the memory-bandwidth limit, so they are excluded. Its points here are model estimates.
  • iMacs are excluded because their wall power includes the built-in display.
  • Hollow points were measured with Cinebench R23 and converted to Cinebench 2024 units through the M4 Mac mini, which was measured in both. Five machines scored in both put the conversion error at about % for all-core and % for single-thread work.
  • Error bars on measured points span the 1st percentile to the maximum of instantaneous wall power during the run, so they overstate the uncertainty of the average.
