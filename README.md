# Apple Silicon work per joule

An interactive, single-file page that compares how much computing each Apple Silicon Mac gets out of a joule taken from the wall. It covers eight workloads: CPU all cores, CPU one thread, linear algebra (HPL, plus matrix-multiply sets with Accelerate, OpenBLAS and Metal), gaming (Cyberpunk 2077, Baldur's Gate 3, Assassin's Creed Shadows), Blender CPU and Blender GPU (one Blender tab, with a Scene menu) and LLM generation. Twenty-two Intel, AMD, Qualcomm and NVIDIA machines with wall readings (plus speed-only DGX Spark runs with estimated power) are shown alongside for comparison, and server-class CPUs (AMD EPYC, Intel Xeon, NVIDIA Grace, Ampere) where their power can be cited: SPECpower_ssj2008, HPL (Geerling's Ampere runs and per-node shares of CPU-only TOP500 systems), one AmpereOne LLM run and Blender CPU medians. The Linear algebra tab has a Set menu: HPL (wall power metered, the default) and three GEMM sets whose power is estimated; only HPL is used in the All view and the matrix. Every point carries uncertainties, and every point can be compared against a chosen baseline machine (the page opens on LLM generation with no baseline, in absolute units).

**Live page:** https://tonyxtyan.github.io/Apple-Silicon-Pareto-Frontier/

## Data model (one row per machine and workload)

- `w`: workload (`cpu-all`, `cpu-one`, `hpl`, `gpu`, `bl-cpu`, `bl-gpu`, `llm`, and `blas` for the GEMM sets of the Linear algebra tab).
- `x`: throughput. `e`: work per joule. `p`: wall power in W. Bounds are given by `xlo/xhi`, `elo/ehi`, `plo/phi`.
- `est`: an estimate. Hidden unless the "Model estimates" switch is on, except rows with `pe` (speed measured, power estimated), which always show. `brw` (with `pe`): the speed was measured on another machine, named in `brw`; such a row counts as its machine's own reading for baselines and is listed in the baseline menu as "(estimated)". `psrc`: an extra source for the power estimate.
- `bm` (cpu-all and cpu-one rows, Macs and comparison machines): `Geekbench 6`, `Cinebench 2026` or (cpu-all only) `SPECpower_ssj2008`, whose `x` is thousands of ssj_ops at 100% target load; rows without it are Cinebench 2024 (or R23 converted), which is what the matrix and the All view use. `resc` on a `bm` row means a Geekbench 5.5 score converted to 6.7.
- `game` (gpu rows only): `Cyberpunk 2077`, `Baldur's Gate 3` or `Assassin's Creed Shadows`. The matrix and the All view use Cyberpunk only.
- `set` and `lib` (blas rows only): `julia-sgemm`, `arxiv-sgemm` or `julia-dgemm`, and the library (`OpenBLAS`, `Accelerate`, `Accelerate (CPU)`, `MPS (GPU)`, `DGEMM fp64`). Pairs on one machine share `grp`, ordered OpenBLAS→Accelerate and CPU→GPU.
- `scene` (bl-cpu and bl-gpu rows only): `monster`, `junkshop` or `classroom`; rows without it are the three-scene sum, which is what the matrix and the All view use. Blender rows also carry `grp`/`ord` so each chip's CPU and GPU point are joined.
- `tier`: `base`, `Pro`, `Max`, `Ultra` for Apple chips; the vendor (`Intel`, `AMD`, `Qualcomm`, `NVIDIA`, `Ampere`) for comparison chips, whose names start with the vendor and count as generation 0. Comparison rows also carry `sl`/`sd`, short chip and machine labels for the plot.
- `form`: `laptop`, `fanless`, `desktop` or `server` (the marker shape). `srv`: a row from server.py; such rows are kept out of the matrix and the All workloads view. `sys`: power is a per-node share of a TOP500/Green500 system (drawn hollow, graded 'B').
- `resc`: the score was converted from Cinebench R23 (drawn hollow). `q`: quality grade; a measured row below 'A', or a speed-measured `pe` row graded 'B' or 'C' (for example a Blender median over three runs or fewer), gets a "weaker measurement" ring. Plain estimates are 'E'.
- LLM extras:
  - `model`: model name.
  - `set`: `sweep`, `mlx`, `cluster`, `omlx` (oMLX Llama 3.2 3B and 1B, gpt-oss 20B, Qwen3 14B, Qwen3.5 27B), `omlxq` (oMLX small Qwen sets), `omlxl` (other oMLX mid-size sets) or `lcpp` (llama.cpp #15396 gpt-oss 20B, `eng` llama.cpp). These sets are kept out of the All view; `omlxq` and `omlxl` are also kept out of the every-model view of the LLM tab and shown only under Other small models and Other mid-size models.
  - `grp`/`ord`: joined by lines in order.
  - `ctx`, `eng`, `nodes`.

Units are chosen so that x / e = wall power in watts. That is why the constant-power diagonals work: Blender uses samples/s (the Open Data score ÷ 60).

## Key modelling choices (details in each point's note)

- **Measured wall power:** Notebookcheck (external monitor) for CPU and Cyberpunk; Jeff Geerling for HPL, M3 Ultra LLM and the cluster.
- **Cinebench:** R23 → 2024 is stitched through the M4 Mac mini (about 5–6% rms). The Intel i9 uses the x86 ratio instead.
- **Estimate model:** log e = workload + tier×workload + generation + tier + chip, ridge-regularised. Its leave-one-out rms sets the error bars. Apple's rated maximum power gives a lower bound on e (desktops only).
- **Apple's published power figures (`apple` in the data, model.py):** desktops (every Mac mini and Mac Studio chip on the page): idle and maximum wall power from Apple's support pages 103253 and 102027 (published 21 September 2026; the M1 Mac mini idle is 6.8 W; the M1, M2 and M2 Pro mini maxima are "CPU Max", a CPU-only load), plus sleep and off from the Product Environmental Report. Laptops (every MacBook on the page, and the M4 Max MacBook Pro 14 from its ENERGY STAR record A3185): the report's idle with the display on, sleep and off (115 V column; the 230 V figures, up to about 10% higher, are in each note), the included adapter and the battery from the tech-specs page. Points show them as "Apple: idle 4.07 W (display on), sleep …, 96 W adapter, 72.4 Wh battery" for laptops and "Apple: idle 7 W, max 50 W (support page); sleep …, off …" for desktops, so the two idle definitions (support-page idle vs report idle with display on, which disagree: 7 W vs 3.96 W for the M2 mini) are never mixed; the readout links the sources and adds notes. Flagged: the 14-inch M3 Max uses the M3 Pro table (the report has none for it); the 14-inch M4 and M4 Pro tables and the M4 Mac mini table repeat the earlier M3, M3 Pro and M2 tables; several tables cover a Pro and a Max chip; the M4 Pro mini figure has no core count and no per-mode split. Clusters and comparison machines get none. Only the desktop maximum bounds anything; the laptop adapter is not a bound, because laptops can draw from the battery beyond it.
- **LLM decode power:** 0.75–1.1 × GPU-load wall power, calibrated on the M4 and M6 Mac mini.
- **Blender power:**
  - CPU: 0.9–1.1 × Cinebench wall power.
  - GPU: 0.45–0.95 × Cyberpunk wall power, anchored only by Eclectic Light's ~20 W GPU reading.
  - Scenes: per-scene medians (monster, junkshop, classroom) from the full Open Data snapshot (Blender 4.5.0, CC0, generated 2026-11-21); each scene reuses the power of the three-scene point. Their sum is within about 2% of the published three-scene median (the median of a sum is not the sum of medians). GPU points are each chip's full-core GPU (the 8-core M2 where the borrowed power is the 8-core MacBook Air). Cut-down GPU variants in Open Data (M1 7c, M1 Pro 14c, M1 Max 24c, M1 Ultra 48c, M2 Pro 16c, M2 Max 30c, M2 Ultra 60c, M3 8c, M3 Pro 14c, M3 Max 30c, M3 Ultra 60c, M4 8c, M4 Pro 16c, M4 Max 32c, M5 Pro 16c, M5 Max 32c) are left out: no machine with them has a measured or modelled power here, and the chip names on the page encode CPU, not GPU, core counts. Medians over three runs or fewer are graded 'B' (M5 Ultra and M6 on both devices, M5 Pro GPU).
- **Comparison machines (x86.py):** the 13-machine shortlist of the research (Framework Desktop and ROG Flow Z13 with the Ryzen AI Max+ 395; Dell Pro Max with GB10; Geerling's Core Ultra 7 265K + RTX 4090 desktop; Zenbook S 14 with the Core Ultra 7 258V; Zenbook S 16 with the Ryzen AI 9 HX 370; XMG Evo 15 with the Core Ultra 7 155H; VIA 14 Pro with the Ryzen 7 8845HS; Minisforum M2 Pro with the Core Ultra X7 358H; Galaxy Book4 Edge 16 with the Snapdragon X Elite X1E-84-100; XMG Neo 16 E25 with the Core Ultra 9 275HX and RTX 5090 Laptop; ProArt P16 with the HX 370 and RTX 5090 Laptop; Dell XPS 13 with the Core 5 320), with every workload each has: Notebookcheck Cinebench 2024 and Cyberpunk 2077 wall power (external monitor), Geerling HPL (average wall power) and LLM runs (peak wall power, so lower bounds, graded 'B' like the M3 Ultra runs). The chip is the processor doing the work, so a discrete-GPU machine's Cyberpunk and LLM rows sit under its NVIDIA GPU and its CPU rows under its Intel or AMD CPU; that wall power still covers the whole laptop or PC. Rows whose power is a stand-in from another test (Framework Desktop and XMG Neo 16 E25 Cinebench) are graded 'B'. Some Cyberpunk frame rates are the review's own 1080p ultra result, which runs 5–15% above the efficiency-chart basis used for the Macs (noted per point); the Snapdragon runs Cyberpunk under emulation. Blender Open Data medians (Blender 4.5.0) for these chips use the Apple rules (0.9–1.1× Cinebench, 0.45–0.95× Cyberpunk wall power) with the research's proxy machine where it is on the page, otherwise the shortlisted machine with that chip; the 275HX (Cinebench power itself a stand-in), the 265K (HPL power stands in) and the desktop RTX 4090 (power from Notebookcheck's own 9800X3D test PC) are graded 'B'. They appear only in the three-scene view of the Blender tab, except the Snapdragon X Elite (blender_arm.py adds its per-scene rows). Left out here (nv2.py later adds the Snapdragon's X1E-84 Blender median and four RTX 4060 laptops): the 14900HX, 285H, 285K and 9950X machines and the RTX 4080/5070/5080 laptops, which are not in the shortlist; the Framework 13 (the shortlist allows the XPS 13 or the Framework 13); and the TDP sweeps (noted on the HX 370, 8845HS and 155H points). No comparison machine is in the Llama 2 7B Q4_0 all-chip set; the RTX 4090 ran Q4_K_M.
- **GEMM sets (Linear algebra tab, numerics.py):** no wall power was metered for any run, and each speed is one community or paper run, so every point is `pe`. Power = 0.5–1.4× (log-centred 0.84) the Cinebench 2024 all-core wall power on this page of the same chip: the named machine where the source names one (Hubner et al.: M1 and M3 MacBook Air 13, M2 and M4 Mac mini), otherwise the chip's best-measured machine (Cinebench 2024 before R23-converted rows, then the narrowest power range; the M3 Ultra uses its model estimate). The band is wide because Accelerate GEMM stops scaling after about two threads (AppleAccelerate.jl benchmarks), which argues for less than Cinebench power, while HPL draws 1.1–1.4× Cinebench power on the M4 Mac mini and M1 Max Mac Studio. 5% is allowed for run-to-run speed spread. The main set is the Julia Discourse AppleNeuralEngine.jl thread (https://discourse.julialang.org/t/-/140344), fp32 GEMM at 2048³ (the size every chip ran; 1024³ figures are in the source too) with Accelerate and OpenBLAS on ten chips; both libraries get the same power, so their efficiency ratio is their speed ratio. The second set is Hubner et al., arXiv 2502.05317, peak SGEMM with Accelerate on the CPU and MPS on the GPU on the M1–M4 base chips; their powermetrics package efficiency (0.2–0.46 TFLOPS/W) is an upper bound and is quoted in each note. The third is three fp64 DGEMM runs on the performance cores (Julia Discourse thread 128459). Left out: MATLAB and Mathematica results (benchmarks in images, or scores confounded by version and kernel count), PyTorch matmul lists with anomalous or unlabelled devices, single-chip sets, and the Ryzen 9 9950X DGEMM reference.
- **Clusters:**
  - exo (tensor parallel): N × 110–240 W.
  - llama.cpp RPC (layer split): one busy node plus (N−1) idle nodes.
- **CPU benchmark menu (cpubench.py):** Notebookcheck Geekbench 6.7 single- and multi-core scores of the same review units whose Cinebench wall power is on the page (22 machines). The other ten Apple machines (M1 and M2 era, and the M3 Max MacBook Pro 16) only have Geekbench 5.5 and are converted with the geometric-mean 6.7/5.5 ratio of the six machines Notebookcheck ran in both (multi-core 1.032, scatter 8%; single-core 1.349, scatter 1.3%), drawn hollow with the scatter added in quadrature to the error bars; larger chips gain less from 5.5 to 6.7 in multi-core (Max about 0.92 to 0.96, base about 1.1), as each note says. Cinebench 2026 has five machines (M5 Air 15, M5 Pro MacBook Pro 16, M5 Max 14 and 16, M4 Pro 12-core MacBook Pro 14). No wall power was published for either, so all rows are `pe`: single-core uses the machine's Cinebench single-thread wall power (range widened by 10%), Geekbench multi-core 0.8–0.95× (log-centred 0.87) its Cinebench all-core wall power, Cinebench 2026 multi-core the Cinebench 2024 wall power (range widened by 10%); 2% is allowed for run-to-run speed noise. The two M4 Pro 12-core MacBook Pro 14 units in Notebookcheck's list are paired by listing order. Left out: the Geekbench Browser per-model averages (version unverified, values from search summaries), Cinebench R20 (the R23 engine the page already converts, mostly the same M1/M2 machines) and Blender 3.3 Classroom (three machines).
- **CPU benchmark menu, comparison machines (cpubench2.py, cpubench2_rows.json):** read 2026-11-21. Notebookcheck Geekbench 6.7 single- and multi-core scores for the Intel, AMD, Qualcomm and NVIDIA review units whose Cinebench wall power is on the page, each checked against that unit's Cinebench 2024 score: Ryzen 7 8845HS Schenker VIA 14 Pro (two runs listed, geometric mean, the spread added to the error bars), Ryzen AI 9 HX 370 ProArt P16 H7606WI (the Strix Point analysis unit, listed in the M5 analysis; not the later RTX 5090 ProArt P16) and Zenbook S 16, Ryzen AI Max+ 395 ROG Flow Z13 and Framework Desktop (multi-core only: the page has no single-thread wall power for it), Core Ultra 7 155H XMG Evo 15, Core Ultra 7 258V Zenbook S 14, Core Ultra 9 275HX XMG Neo 16 E25 (not the Oasis liquid-cooled configuration), Core Ultra X7 358H Minisforum M2 Pro (Balanced default mode, as its Cinebench row), both NVIDIA N1X (RTX Spark) laptops and the Snapdragon X Elite Galaxy Book4 Edge 16: 12 multi-core and 11 single-core points. Cinebench 2026 (multi and single) is added for the three that ran it: the Minisforum M2 Pro and the two N1X laptops. Every unit ran Geekbench 6.7, so none is converted from 5.5 (the 5.5 scores are kept in the rows file). Power is borrowed as in cpubench.py (single-core: Cinebench 2024 single-thread wall power widened 10%; Geekbench multi-core: 0.8–0.95× the Cinebench 2024 all-core wall power; Cinebench 2026: the Cinebench 2024 power widened 10%), all rows `q` 'E', `est`, `pe`. Where a machine's Cinebench run peaks more than 1.3× its average (a high short-term power limit: the Zenbook S 14, Galaxy Book4 Edge 16 and Minisforum M2 Pro), Geekbench's short bursts may draw nearer the peak than the factor assumes; the reviews give no Geekbench power reading, so the factor is kept and those notes say so. Left out: the i9-9880H MacBook Pro 16 (2019), whose review predates Geekbench 5.5 and 6 and Cinebench 2026 (it has none of them, and no other Notebookcheck review of that machine and chip was found; its single-thread power is itself an assumption).
- **Small models (llm4.py):** crowd-sourced oMLX Community Benchmarks (MLX, 1k-token context, scraped 2026-11-21), `tok_s_robust` (the median of the runs within 0.6× of the best run in a chip and GPU-core bin). Llama 3.2 3B 4-bit (18 chips) and 1B 4-bit (17 chips), plus Qwen2.5 0.5B, Qwen3 0.6B, Qwen3 1.7B and Qwen2.5 3B (4-bit, 11 to 14 chips). Qwen3 1.7B 8-bit (13 chips, 12 of them single runs), Llama 3.2 3B 8-bit and Phi-4-mini (8 chips each) are left out. Where oMLX splits a chip by GPU core count, the bin matching the chip's Llama 2 7B point (llm.py) is kept and the others are dropped (named in each point's note); where only a cut-down bin exists (M1 Ultra 48-core and M3 Ultra 60-core with 1B, M2 Ultra 60-core with 3B, the M2 8-core and M4 Max 32-core in some Qwen sets) it is kept and the note says the power estimate is for the full GPU. Power is the chip's Llama 2 7B decode-power estimate, widened by 10% (as for the XiongjieDai sweeps), so all rows are `pe`; small models stream fewer bytes per token, so efficiency is more likely understated. Speed spread is 0.04 + 0.15/√n on a log scale; bins with one or two runs are graded 'B'. Caveats: runs mix oMLX v0.2.6 to v0.7.x and macOS 15.7 to 27, oMLX does not record the chassis, and versions shift results (the M5 Pro 20-core 3B bin, kept, is slower than the 16-core bin, partly from version changes). MLX speeds are not comparable with the llama.cpp points.
- **Mid-size models (llm5.py, omlx_mid_rows.json):** oMLX Community Benchmarks (MLX 4-bit, 1k-token context, scraped 2026-11-21) with `tok_s_robust`, here the median of the runs within 0.7 to 1.3× the bin median, after a tier-aware ceiling dropped runs faster per GB/s of rated bandwidth than 1.6× their tier's median (speculative decoding; 42 Qwen3-Coder, 58 Qwen3.5 35B-A3B and 13 Gemma 4 31B runs). Bins flagged `suspect` (more than 40% off the tier's bandwidth-scaled median) are skipped, and the Gemma 4 31B M5 bin (16.4 tok/s, about twice what 153 GB/s can stream) is left out by hand. gpt-oss 20B (MXFP4 experts, 19 chips), Qwen3 14B (19) and Qwen3.5 27B (17; base and a same-architecture fine-tune pooled) are `omlx` sets with their own Model-menu entries; every other oMLX set with eight chips or more is `omlxl` (Qwen3 30B-A3B, Qwen3-Coder 30B-A3B, Qwen3.5 and 3.6 35B-A3B, Qwen3.6 and 3.8 27B, Qwen3 32B, Qwen2.5 14B and 32B, Qwen2.5-Coder 32B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen 14B and 32B, Devstral Small 2 24B, Mistral Small 24B, Gemma 3 12B and 27B, Gemma 4 31B and 26B-A4B: 9 to 18 chips each, 254 points); Phi-4, QwQ and Mistral Nemo (4 to 5 chips) are left out. Model names match rows already on the page (gpt-oss 20B, DeepSeek R1 14B, Qwen3 32B, Qwen3 30B-A3B, Qwen3.5 35B-A3B 4-bit) plus ' (oMLX)', so each shares a frontier with them. GPU-core bins, power (the chip's Llama 2 7B decode estimate widened by 10%, `pe`), speed spread and grading follow llm4.py. The llama.cpp gpt-oss guide thread (discussion #15396; llama-bench tg128, MXFP4 GGUF, seven chips, one post each, builds 6123 to 6310) is a `lcpp` set under the model name gpt-oss 20B with the same power rule, graded 'B' (the M3 Max post, which Georgi Gerganov judged heat-throttled, 'C'); the M4 Max post is a 32-core GPU and three posts give no core count. Measured: Jeff Geerling's M3 Ultra Qwen3 32B (26.69 tok/s; his file is named Qwen3-30B-A3B but llama-bench reports the dense 32B) and Llama 2 13B (61.39) at his 227 W peak, graded 'B' like his other rows (his R1 14B and gpt-oss 20B rows were already here; his M1 Ultra power is from iStat, not the wall, so it is not used); an M4 Pro Mac mini running Qwen3 32B on Ollama at 11.7 tok/s and '~40 W under load' (archy.net, method unstated, 'C'). Tom's Hardware's M5 Ultra reading (about 250 W from 138.96 kJ in 555.2 s) is cited in the note but not plotted: it is an agentic, prefill-heavy run with multi-token prediction on, not plain decoding. No source metered several chips on a mid-size model; the YouTube wall-meter videos could not be read as text.
- **Small variants of the newer families (llm6.py, omlx_more_rows.json):** oMLX Community Benchmarks (MLX 4-bit, 1k-token context, scraped 2026-11-21), the same `tok_s_robust` median (runs within 0.7 to 1.3× the bin median), detail-page checks (runs with MTP, DFlash, SpecPrefill, TurboQuant or a non-Nominal thermal state dropped), tier-aware ceiling (1.6× the tier's median tok/s per GB/s of rated bandwidth) and `suspect` bins as for the mid-size sets. Only plain 4-bit builds of the base model were taken (fine-tunes, abliterated, oQ/OptiQ/MTP quantizations are excluded by name); for the popular models only the most recent 400 runs per chip variant were read, so a bin can be missing runs from before mid-2026. llm6.py adds a bandwidth check: a bin is dropped if its tok/s times a lower bound on the GB of weights streamed per token (0.4 GB for 0.8B up to 6 GB for Gemma 4 12B) exceeds the chip's rated memory bandwidth; nothing tripped it (the fastest bins reach 0.82 of the bound). ## Page features

- **Tabs and baseline:** one tab per workload plus All workloads; Blender CPU and GPU share one tab, and HPL and the GEMM sets share the Linear algebra tab. The baseline menu switches to ratios; None gives absolute units. The page opens on LLM generation with no baseline; the first time another tab is shown (by a click, a link or a hash), the baseline becomes the M4 Max MacBook Pro 14 (estimated), unless the user has already chosen a baseline themselves (the baseline menu, a matrix row, Use as baseline or Clear baseline, None included). On tabs where that machine has no row (Linear algebra, Blender, LLM, Geekbench and Cinebench 2026), the baseline falls back to the M4 Max's best row on that workload (the Mac Studio for HPL, the MacBook Pro 16 elsewhere).
- **Blender tab:** CPU renders are dashed rings, GPU renders are filled, and a thin line joins each chip's pair. A **Scene** menu shows the three-scene sum (the default, as in the matrix) or one scene; a **Device** menu shows CPU, GPU or both. Frontiers are per device; baselines compare the same scene on the same device. Old `#bl-cpu` and `#bl-gpu` links open the tab on that device.
- **All workloads with None:** stacked per-workload scales.
- **Display menu (top right):** theme (auto, light, dark), text size (90 to 130%) and typeface (Archivo, system, Atkinson Hyperlegible, Source Serif 4, compact (Archivo at 78% width), JetBrains Mono), remembered in this browser.
- **All workloads scales:** one workload's scale at a time, picked from the Scale row (hover previews it and highlights its points; click keeps it); "Show every scale" restores the side-by-side scales when there is room, and hovering any scale highlights its points.
- **Option hover:** hovering a chip-tier or machine option highlights those points on the plot.
- **Hover:** shows relatives and the exact power line. Click a point to pin it. Hover highlights (any combination, each with its own line style): related chips (solid, dashed for other generations), same chip (dotted), same chip and machine (thick pale), same benchmark (thin blue) (same model, game, scene and device, set and library, or workload).
- **Chart navigation:** zoom, pan and pinch; full screen.
- **Speed vs power view:** a button in the chart toolbar swaps the axes to the chart Apple usually shows: wall power on x, speed on y, so up and to the left is better. Points slide between the two layouts (no animation with reduced motion); the frontier there joins the points no other point beats on both lower power and higher speed, the diagonal guides are lines of equal work per joule, and the hover crosshair reads power and speed. Both axes are absolute, so a baseline only affects the default view; the button is off on All workloads, which has no single speed unit.
- **Efficiency frontier:** straight Pareto segments with fitted ends (exponential by default; also power law, log or linear). It is drawn separately for each workload, each LLM model, each game and each Blender device. Every group of two or more points gets its line, including a pair where one point wins (drawn as that point's corner to the axes). Every frontier is named: names for lines that reach the left axis form one evenly spaced column there, each joined to its line by a short leader, and the rest sit beside their first frontier point. Point labels steer around them. Hovering a name brings its frontier forward and fades the others. On phones every line is drawn but only the eight largest groups are named.
- **Matrix:** chip × workload, with estimates and ratio ± uncertainty. A hatched **n/a** cell cannot exist, and its tooltip says why; a – cell is simply not measured. The rules (`NA` in template.html): a discrete NVIDIA GeForce card (desktop or laptop) has no CPU all-core, CPU one-thread, HPL or Blender CPU cell, because the CPU results of those machines sit under their Intel or AMD processor; the NVIDIA GB10 has no Cinebench (all-core, one-thread) or Cyberpunk cell, because its machines run DGX OS (Linux on Arm), for which neither has a build; a Qualcomm Snapdragon has no Blender GPU cell, because Cycles has no GPU backend for Adreno. A rule never hides a value: a cell with data shows it.
- **Leave-one-out plot:** shows both error bars; drawn large beside the method notes on wide screens, compact on phones.
- **Linear algebra tab and its Set menu:** HPL and the former Numerical libraries tab share one tab (`hpl` pools the `hpl` and `blas` rows, as Blender pools CPU and GPU). Sets: HPL, fp64 (metered; the default, including the comparison machines), fp64 DGEMM (Julia, placed next to HPL because it is the same precision), fp32 GEMM Accelerate vs OpenBLAS, peak SGEMM CPU vs GPU, or every set. Frontiers and frontier names are per set and library (HPL is one group); baselines compare the same set and library (the HPL set keeps ratio axes, the GEMM sets and every set keep absolute axes with ratios in each point's details). HPL reports Gflops and the GEMM sets GFLOPS, the same unit, but fp64 and fp32 operations are different work. Only HPL is metered; it stays the linear-algebra column of the matrix and the All view. Old `#hpl` links open the HPL set and old `#blas` links open the fp32 Accelerate vs OpenBLAS set.
- **Benchmark menu (CPU tabs):** Cinebench 2024 (default, power measured), Geekbench 6, Cinebench 2026 or, on the all-cores tab only, SPECpower_ssj2008 (servers only, AC wall power measured; axis in thousand ssj_ops and thousand ssj_ops per joule; a server baseline as described under Server-class CPUs). There is no every-benchmark view, because scores are on different scales. Axis titles, finding and note follow the benchmark; a baseline divides by the baseline machine in the same benchmark; the matrix and All workloads stay on Cinebench 2024.
- **Game menu (gaming tab):** every game, or one. Frontiers are drawn per game. A baseline divides each game by that machine in the same game, so the baseline sits at 1× in every game; with every game shown, frame rate stays absolute and the constant-power lines are hidden (they differ per game). Games the baseline has no reading for are dropped while it is set.
- **LLM Model menu:** a Collections group (every model and context, every-chip sets, context sweeps, MLX vs GGUF, clusters, other metered runs) and then every model on its own, grouped by size class with its chip count. Earlier text: 7B all-chip set, Llama 3.2 3B and 1B 4-bit on every chip (oMLX), Other small models (oMLX Qwen sets), gpt-oss 20B, Qwen3 14B and Qwen3.5 27B 4-bit on every chip (oMLX; the gpt-oss entry also shows the llama.cpp #15396 set and the metered gpt-oss runs), Other mid-size models (oMLX), context sweeps, MLX vs GGUF, clusters. With one oMLX model chosen, a baseline compares the same model. **Model size** chips filter by total parameters: XS <2B, S 2–5B, M 5–12B, L 12–50B, XL 50–150B, XXL 150–500B, XXXL ≥500B.
- **Palette:** colours come from the dataviz skill's validated palette. Intel is #c2418f in light mode and #b84a8c in dark; in light mode Pro is #e05a1a and Max is darkened to #2a925c for 3:1 contrast on the white plot field. The comparison vendors were found by a search over hues, scored with the dataviz validator's OKLab metric against the five existing colours and each other in both themes: AMD brick #943207 / brown #85552e, Qualcomm violet #430ceb / #8c0cf5, NVIDIA purple #7c0899 / pink #f525cb (light / dark). Ampere was found the same way against all eight: lilac #a288ca in light mode (the only region of the light hue space left: normal-vision ΔE 15.5, protan/deutan 8.6, tritan 13.7, contrast 3.04 on the white field, OKLCH C 0.10) and gold #c9b708 in dark mode (ΔE 21.1, protan/deutan 14.6, tritan 15.7, contrast 9.2; above the dark lightness band, like the Ultra token, because no in-band hue clears the gates). Every pair involving a new colour keeps normal-vision ΔE ≥ 15.0, protan/deutan ΔE ≥ 8.3 and tritan ΔE ≥ 6.7 (OKLab ×100), no worse than the existing Apple pairs (Intel–Max 6.8 protan/deutan, base–Max 4.0 tritan), and contrast on the plot field is ≥ 3:1 in both themes (dark AMD 3.01, dark Qualcomm 3.14). With nine colours a scatter cannot pass every all-pairs gate, so the chip-tier chips, labels and table names carry the vendor too.
- **Machine (shape) chips:** laptop with fan (circle), fanless laptop (triangle), desktop (square) and server (hexagon). Hovering one highlights those points; a click shows only that form (with every form shown), later clicks add or remove forms, and a fading Show all restores them.
- **Comparison chips:** the chip-tier chips include Intel, AMD, Qualcomm, NVIDIA and Ampere; the baseline menu lists them in one group per vendor; the matrix lists them after the Apple chips under a divider; hovering one draws lines to the other chips of the same vendor.
